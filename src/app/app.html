Chapter 01

The basics.

Etiquette is not about perfection. It is about making everyone around you feel comfortable, respected, and included.

01 / Why it matters

Professionalism
has a place setting.

Business dining etiquette is the set of considerate habits that help people share a meal while working toward a professional goal. It includes preparation, table manners, conversation, and awareness of the people around you.

At a meal, your choices communicate before you say a word. Arriving prepared, listening with care, and treating staff respectfully signal reliability and maturity—qualities that matter in every career.

“The goal is not to perform etiquette. The goal is to create room for a good conversation.”
01

Respect

Consider the host, your guests, the staff, and the shared pace of the table.

02

Confidence

Preparation turns unfamiliar situations into opportunities to connect.

03

Professionalism

Bring the same thoughtful judgment to a meal that you bring to your work.

Chapter 02

Before
the dinner.

The best business dinners begin before you reach the restaurant.

Your preparation checklist

Arrive ready
to be present.

Dress with intention

When in doubt, choose polished and slightly more formal. Your clothing should support the conversation, not become the conversation.

Know the roles

The host typically chooses the venue and guides timing. Guests can be gracious, flexible, and mindful of the agreed price range.

Read the room

A quiet interview dinner calls for a different energy than a celebratory team meal. Let the purpose guide your pace.

Chapter 03

At the table.

Simple signals make a shared meal feel effortless. Use these as guides, not tests.

PLATE
FORK
KNIFE
SPOON
GLASS
BREAD

The place setting

When in doubt,
work outside in.

Forks sit to the left, knives and spoons to the right. Bread is above your forks; your water glass is above the knife. Start with the utensils farthest from your plate.

Good to know

Napkin

Place it on your lap after the host does. If you step away, leave it on your chair; at the end, place it loosely to the left of your plate.

Posture & pace

Keep both feet grounded, elbows relaxed, and your pace close to the group. Take small bites and wait until you have swallowed before speaking.

Unexpected moments

If you drop a utensil, ask the server quietly for a replacement. If you dislike a dish, stay discreet and focus on the company.

Phone

Keep it out of sight. A meal is shared attention; step away only for a genuine emergency.

Chapter 04

Conversation
& connection.

The most memorable guest is not the loudest one. Be curious, generous, and present.

A simple rhythm

Ask. Listen.
Build trust.

Good topics

Ask about a project, a recent professional accomplishment, a shared interest, or what drew someone to their field. Open-ended questions invite real answers.

Topics to save for later

Avoid politics, personal finances, gossip, and comments about someone’s appearance. When unsure, redirect toward shared professional interests.

Try saying

“What has been the most interesting part of your work this year?”

To close well

Thank the host, mention a specific part of the conversation, and confirm any next step before you leave.
